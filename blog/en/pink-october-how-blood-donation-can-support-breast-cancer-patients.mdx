Pink October or Breast cancer awareness month is more than just an annual awareness campaign. It’s a month dedicated to women, and an opportunity to learn about one of their struggles, a time to start conversations that are difficult to have and to remind ourselves. Behind every diagnostic, there is a woman a person, and a life that matters. And understanding these people begins with understanding the reality of breast cancer itself.

Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed cancer among women in Morocco. According to the international Agency for research on cancer (IARC), an estimated 10.410 new cases were recorded among moroccan women in 2024, representing approximately 39% of all new cancer cases among women that year, and many more go undiagnosed. But let’s not forget, these are not just numbers, they are real people, whose lives changed overnight.

Breast cancer develops when abnormal cells in the breast tissue grow uncontrollably, forming a malignant tumor. And when it goes untreated or undiagnosed, it starts invading the rest of the breast tissue and cancer starts spreading to the rest of the body, slowly killing healthy cells and then the body. And that’s why early detection matters, checking your body and seeking medical advice about unusual changes can help identify potential warning signs, such as:

- A new lump or mass in the breast or armpit (most lumps are not cancer, but all new ones should be checked);
- Thickening or swelling of part of the breast;
- Irritation, dimpling, or redness of the breast skin;
- Pulling in of the nipple or pain in the nipple area;
- Spontaneous nipple discharge.

These changes do not automatically mean cancer, but they should not be ignored. And while receiving a diagnosis can be life changing and devastating, fear not, because this is not the end of the story. Treatment is available, and support can make a big difference.

### This is where we need your help.

Some breast cancer patients undergoing treatment might need blood transfusions. Chemotherapy can affect blood cells production, sometimes causing anemia or reducing the number of platelets, while surgery and certain complications can also create a need for donated blood. Although not every breast cancer patient requires transfusion, access to safe blood can be an important part of their care. In Morocco, the need for blood is ongoing. The Moroccan Agency for Blood and Its Derivatives has emphasized the importance of regular blood donation to meet the needs of patients with cancer and other medical conditions.

The percentage of the population regularly donating blood sits at approximately 0.92%, which falls just short of the World Health Organization's (WHO) recommended minimum threshold of 1% to 2%. Meanwhile, moroccans need more than 1,000 blood donations to meet the needs of hospitals, emergency departments, and surgical units across the country. Yet regular blood donation remains a challenge. And behind these numbers are people waiting for treatment, families hoping for recovery, and patients whose care may depend on the availability of donated blood. By donating blood regularly, we can help strengthen Morocco's blood supply and ensure that more patients have access to the care they need.

### So what can we do this Pink October?

We can do more than wear a pink ribbon. We can educate ourselves, share reliable information, encourage the people around us to seek medical advice when something concerns them, and offer compassion to those affected by breast cancer. And we can turn that compassion into action by donating blood and encouraging others to do the same. Your donation could help save up to three lives. So why not take that step today?
